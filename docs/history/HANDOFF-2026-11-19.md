## Resume results

- Both B70s returned at `03:00.0` and `07:00.0`. The guard started before GPU tests.
- `tools/kv_vmm_probe.cpp` passed on both B70s. Level Zero reported 64 KiB minimum and recommended pages. Both
  64 KiB and 2 MiB allocations supported cross-page GPU loads and physical page reuse at a different virtual
  address. The engine allocator test preserved data across growth, rejected a second slot when the budget was
  full, and gave the freed budget to the second slot after retirement.
- `GRIMOIRE_KV_POOL=1` is the experimental single-GPU key-major FP8 path. Admission backs the prompt and the
  requested generation budget from shared VRAM. Cached prefixes stay mapped and can be evicted when another
  request needs space. Uncached slots release their mappings at retirement. The Flash-Next candidate adds
  key-major sparse attention and pools the QSA indexer and single-slot MTP head.
- Dense backbone A/B, Swift Qwen3.8 GPTQ INT4 with MTP K6 on one B70, four slots, 32K context: 24/24 copybench
  texts and 5/5 repeated/extended-prefix texts were byte-identical. The fixed cache allocated 4 GiB at load.
  The pooled run reported an 11.80 GiB shared ceiling and backed requests on admission. Median decode changed
  −0.06% at one user and −0.20% at two users. Median first-token time rose 29 ms and 69 ms respectively.
  At approximately 6K prompt tokens, first-token time was 2.19/2.21 s cold, 11/10 ms for the exact repeat,
  and 106/103 ms for the next turn (fixed/pooled).
- With the dense MTP head also in the pool, the 24/24 comparison still matched. The Flash-Next QSA indexer's
  compressed block-boundary probe passed on both B70s.
- Eight concurrent Qwen requests with `--ctx 131072`, Swift INT4 and MTP K6 on one B70: fixed allocation fit two
  slots, while pooling admitted eight short requests. Estimated aggregate throughput from the slowest request's
  completion time rose from 153 to 315 tok/s for code and from 124 to 284 tok/s for essays (two repeats each).
  The requests used short prompts and generated 128 tokens. Text matched in 27 of 32 comparisons across
  the two batch widths.
- Flash-Next INT8 on one B70, 6,064-token copy prompt and 128 generated tokens: pooled key-major KV improved
  38.52 → 40.22 tok/s with MTP off and 30.82 → 34.03 tok/s with MTP K3. The copy text matched byte for byte
  in both comparisons. Free-writing text changed with pooled key-major KV in both comparisons. These are
  single runs per setting, so they do not establish a general speedup or substitute for the full regression.
- Rejected the direct BAR-read exchange. The next Flash-Next hyper-connection read the reduced values from the BAR
  buffer after the write fence and readback. Both plain and MTP matched all eight texts across the switch, but
  plain decode did not get faster. The short story's MTP step took 37.96 ms with the existing exchange (33.69 ms
  verify) and 39.71 ms with the experiment. The experiment remains in `f118fae` and Tower's `exchange` build.
- The existing expert-hit snapshot favored rank 0 by 1.3% for cold-expert touches. The best static share
  in a sweep around 50/50 lowered the sum of the predicted per-layer maximum by about 0.5%. Using a two-card
  route trace, a partition based on co-occurrence reduced the sum of per-call maximum cold-expert counts
  from 47,522 to 45,408 on held-out calls (4.4%). A two-card runtime A/B preserved the hot experts and per-rank
  slot counts and changed only cold-expert ownership. With MTP off and a 6K copy prompt, median decode over three
  runs rose from 50.00 to 50.35 tok/s (0.7%, byte-identical outputs). The three free-writing runs had differing
  outputs. The copy measurement is too small to establish a general exchange speedup.
