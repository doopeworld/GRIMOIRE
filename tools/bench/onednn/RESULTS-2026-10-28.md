# Results 2026-10-28 -- Flash-Next on two B70s, decode

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4, Megatron TP over both cards, 170 experts a layer in VRAM per card,
the rest in RAM (CPU experts), PLE table in RAM.  llama-benchy 0.4.0, the client pinned to
CPUs 14-15 in both columns.  Same build; "before" = the four changes switched off
(GRIMOIRE_TOPK_ESIMD=0 GRIMOIRE_TP_HEAD_SPLIT=0 GRIMOIRE_TMOE_LEAN=0 GRIMOIRE_HC_NORM_REGS=0).

| Test | Before | After | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| tg128 after a 2,048-token prompt | 38.47 tok/s | **42.71 tok/s** | +11.0% |
| tg128 after a 4,096-token prompt | 36.36 tok/s | **40.33 tok/s** | +10.9% |
| pp2048 | 1,235 tok/s | 1,348 tok/s | +9.2% |
| pp4096 | 1,453 tok/s | 1,477 tok/s | +1.7% |
| Same answers (3 prompts, frozen expert placement) | yes (twice) | **yes, identical to before** | |

What changed (each is exact):
1. Picking the 10 experts out of 512: a register kernel instead of a slow generic one (43 us -> ~4 us a layer).
2. The output head split between the cards (each scores half the vocabulary, the best of both wins).
3. Expert kernels skip the work-groups and copies of experts that are not on this card's VRAM.
4. The stream-mix norm loads its values up front (it waited for every load in turn).

Step by step (top-k alone): tg128 40.81 / 39.14, pp 1,268 / 1,513.
